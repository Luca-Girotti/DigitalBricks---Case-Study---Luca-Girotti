# Purchasing and Invoicing

Document owner: Finance
Last reviewed: January 2026
Applies to: all Northwind Facilities sites

## Raising a purchase order

Every purchase from an approved supplier requires a purchase order
raised before the goods or services are ordered. The purchase order
number must be quoted to the supplier at the point of order.

A purchase order must reference the ticket the work relates to. Purchase
orders without a ticket reference are rejected by Finance and returned
to the raiser.

Retrospective purchase orders are permitted only for emergency spend
authorised under the emergency provision, and must be raised within two
working days.

## Supplier invoices

Supplier invoices are matched against the purchase order and the ticket
before payment. An invoice that does not match the purchase order value
within a tolerance of 10 percent, or 100 EUR, whichever is lower, is
held for query.

Standard payment terms with approved suppliers are 30 days from invoice
date. Two suppliers hold negotiated terms of 45 days; Finance can
confirm which on request.

## Recharging to the client

Work that falls outside the managed-service contract is recharged to the
client. The ticket must record that the work is chargeable, and the
client's nominated contact must have approved the cost in writing before
the work starts.

Callout charges are recharged only where the fault falls outside the
contract scope, or where the callout was requested by the client for a
fault later found not to exist.

Northwind does not recharge a callout where the engineer was unable to
complete the work because of something within Northwind's control,
including a missing part that should have been held in stock.

## Consumables held in stock

Each site holds a minimum stock of common consumables, including HVAC
filters in the sizes used at that site, lamps, and tap cartridges.

Stock is replenished monthly against the approved supplier for that
category. Items drawn from stock are recorded against the ticket so that
usage can be recharged where applicable.

Stock is not a substitute for a purchase order when an item has to be
ordered specially.
